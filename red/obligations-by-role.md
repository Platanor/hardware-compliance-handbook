**RED obligations by economic-operator role: manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor**

Directive 2014/53/EU (Chapter II, Articles 10–15) assigns distinct sets of obligations to each role in the supply chain. The structure mirrors the pattern used across EU product-harmonisation law (and reused by the CRA): the manufacturer carries the substantive compliance burden, while importers and distributors carry a due-diligence and verification burden appropriate to their more limited visibility into how the product was designed and built.

**Manufacturer (Article 10)**

The manufacturer bears full responsibility for the radio equipment's compliance. Core obligations:

- Ensure the equipment is designed and manufactured to meet the essential requirements of Article 3 (both the unconditional 3(1)–(2) requirements and any activated 3(3) points).
- Ensure the equipment can operate in at least one Member State without infringing radio-spectrum rules.
- Draw up the technical documentation (Article 21) and carry out the applicable conformity assessment procedure (Article 17), or have it carried out.
- Where conformity has been demonstrated, draw up the EU declaration of conformity and affix the CE marking.
- **Retain the technical documentation and the EU declaration of conformity for 10 years** after the equipment is placed on the market.
- Maintain procedures so that series production stays in conformity — taking into account design changes and changes to the harmonised standards relied on.
- Where appropriate given the risk, carry out sample testing, investigate complaints, and keep a register of non-conforming equipment and recalls, keeping distributors informed.
- Mark equipment with a type, batch, or serial number (or equivalent identifier); indicate the manufacturer's name/trade name/trademark and a postal contact address on the equipment, packaging, or accompanying document.
- Accompany the equipment with instructions and safety information in a language end-users can understand, including — for equipment that intentionally emits radio waves — the frequency band(s) and maximum RF power transmitted.
- Ensure each item is accompanied by the (or a simplified) EU declaration of conformity.
- Where the manufacturer becomes aware or has reason to believe equipment it placed on the market is non-conforming, immediately take corrective action (bring into conformity, withdraw, or recall) and, where the equipment presents a risk, immediately inform the competent national authorities of the Member States concerned, with details of the non-compliance and any corrective measures.
- On reasoned request from a competent national authority, provide all information and documentation necessary to demonstrate conformity, in a language that authority can understand, and cooperate on any risk-elimination action.

**Authorised representative (Article 11)**

A manufacturer may appoint an authorised representative by written mandate. The obligation to ensure the design meets essential requirements (Article 10(1)) and the obligation to draw up technical documentation (Article 10(3)) **cannot** be delegated to the authorised representative — those stay with the manufacturer. What the mandate *can* cover: keeping the EU declaration of conformity and technical documentation available to market surveillance authorities for 10 years, providing information/documentation on request, and cooperating with authorities on risk-elimination action.

**Importer (Article 12)**

An importer may only place compliant radio equipment on the market. Before doing so, the importer must verify:

- The manufacturer carried out the appropriate conformity assessment procedure (Article 17).
- The equipment can be operated in at least one Member State without infringing radio-spectrum rules.
- The manufacturer drew up the technical documentation, the equipment bears the CE marking, and it's accompanied by the required information/documents (instructions, safety information, declaration of conformity).
- The manufacturer complied with its marking and contact-information obligations (Article 10(6)–(7)).

If the importer considers or has reason to believe the equipment is non-conforming, it must not place it on the market until it is brought into conformity, and must inform the manufacturer and market surveillance authorities if the equipment presents a risk. Importers must also: indicate their own name/trade name/trademark and postal address on the equipment (or packaging/accompanying document); ensure instructions and safety information are provided in an understandable language; ensure storage/transport conditions under their responsibility don't jeopardise compliance; carry out sample testing/investigate complaints where appropriate; take corrective action and notify authorities if equipment they placed on the market turns out non-conforming; **keep the EU declaration of conformity for 10 years** and ensure technical documentation can be made available to authorities on request; and cooperate with authorities on reasoned requests.

**Distributor (Article 13)**

A distributor must act with due care in relation to RED's requirements. Before making equipment available on the market, a distributor must verify:

- The equipment bears the CE marking.
- It's accompanied by the documents required by the directive, and by instructions/safety information in a language end-users in that Member State can understand.
- The manufacturer and importer have each complied with their own respective marking/documentation obligations.

If the distributor considers or has reason to believe the equipment is non-conforming, it must not make it available until brought into conformity, and must inform the manufacturer/importer and, if the equipment presents a risk, the market surveillance authorities. Distributors must ensure storage/transport conditions under their responsibility don't jeopardise compliance, ensure corrective measures are taken if non-conformity is found in equipment they've already made available, and cooperate with authorities on reasoned requests.

**When an importer or distributor becomes "the manufacturer" (Article 14)**

An importer or distributor is treated as the manufacturer, and takes on the manufacturer's full Article 10 obligations, if it either:

1. Places the radio equipment on the market under its own name or trademark, or
2. Modifies equipment already placed on the market in a way that could affect its compliance with the directive.

This is the same "substantial modification" and "own-brand" logic the CRA uses (see [`obligations-by-role.md`](../cra/obligations-by-role.md)) — white-labelling an OEM product does not shift compliance responsibility away from the brand owner.

**Traceability (Article 15)**

All economic operators must, on request from market surveillance authorities, be able to identify: any operator that supplied them with the equipment, and any operator they supplied it to. This traceability information must be available for **10 years** after the equipment was supplied or received.

**The 10-year figure recurs deliberately.** Technical documentation retention, EU declaration of conformity retention, and supply-chain traceability are all set to a 10-year window under RED — the same order of magnitude (though not an identical mechanism) as the CRA's own technical-documentation retention period.
