**RED penalties and market surveillance**

**No EU-wide fixed fine amounts — unlike the CRA**

This is the most important distinction to get right when comparing RED to the CRA. The CRA sets specific, EU-wide maximum penalty tiers directly in its own text (Article 64: up to €15 million or 2.5% of global turnover for the most serious violations, down to €5 million or 1% for lesser ones — see [`penalties-enforcement.md`](../cra/penalties-enforcement.md)). **RED does not do this.** Article 46 of Directive 2014/53/EU reads, in full:

> "Member States shall lay down rules on penalties applicable to infringements by economic operators of the provisions of national law adopted pursuant to this Directive and shall take all measures necessary to ensure that they are enforced. Such rules may include criminal penalties for serious infringements. The penalties provided for shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive."

RED is a **directive**, not a regulation — it does not apply directly in Member States, it has to be transposed into each country's national law (Article 49 required this by 12 June 2016). Because the penalty regime is left to national transposition, **the actual fine amounts, and whether criminal liability attaches, vary by Member State.** There is no single number to cite for "the RED fine" the way there is for the CRA. Any claim stating a specific euro amount or percentage of turnover as "the RED penalty" without naming which Member State's transposing law it comes from should be treated as unverified.

**What this means practically:** a manufacturer selling radio equipment across multiple EU Member States is potentially exposed to different penalty regimes in each one for the same underlying non-compliance, since each country independently transposed Article 46 into its own law. Checking the specific national transposing legislation for each market is necessary if an exact figure is required — this repository does not hold that country-by-country detail.

**Market surveillance (Chapter V, Articles 39–43)**

RED does not create its own bespoke market-surveillance regime — it incorporates the general EU framework by reference. Article 39 states that Article 15(3) and Articles 16 to 29 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 (the general EU regulation on accreditation and market surveillance) apply to radio equipment. This gives national market surveillance authorities the standard EU toolkit: powers to inspect equipment, demand technical documentation, and order corrective action, withdrawal, or recall where equipment is found non-compliant or presents a risk.

Where technical documentation submitted by a manufacturer or importer doesn't sufficiently demonstrate compliance with Article 3's essential requirements, a market surveillance authority can require testing by a body it accepts, at the manufacturer's or importer's expense, within a specified period (Article 21(4)).

**Improper use of the CE marking**

Article 20(4) requires Member States to build on existing mechanisms to ensure the CE marking regime is applied correctly, and to take appropriate action against improper use of the marking. Affixing a CE mark to equipment that does not actually meet the applicable essential requirements — or to equipment not covered by CE-marking legislation at all — is treated as a compliance failure in its own right, separate from any underlying technical non-conformity. The same principle applies under the CRA (see the "false declaration" discussion in [`cra-red-ce-marking-guide.md`](../cra-red-ce-marking-guide.md)).

**Notified-body oversight**

Where a conformity assessment procedure involves a notified body (Module B+C or H — required where no harmonised standard is applied, or applied only in part, per Article 17(4)), that body is itself subject to ongoing oversight. Notifying authorities can restrict, suspend, or withdraw a body's notification if it no longer meets the competence requirements of Article 26, and the Commission can investigate and require corrective action where a notified body's competence is in doubt (Articles 32–33).

**Review and reporting obligations (Article 47)**

Member States must report to the Commission on RED's application (market surveillance activity, compliance levels) at least every two years. The Commission itself reviews the directive's overall operation and reports to the European Parliament and Council every five years, covering progress on harmonised standards and any implementation problems.
